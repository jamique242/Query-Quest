<h1>📊 Observations from the Analysis</h1>

Overall, the analysis highlights that revenue growth is steady, customer revenue is diversified, discounting materially impacts profitability, and regional performance varies meaningfully across customer spend and margin contribution.

<h3>📈 Revenue & Profit Trends</h3>

Revenue shows consistent growth patterns, in spite of seasonal spikes and drops. Our profit margin fluctuates month-to-month but on average ytd seems to stay above 10% and holds a cummulative average of about 12%, there is no strong indication of profitability increasing. We actually see an overall decline in our last year of sales. Some revenue growth may be margin-dilutive (depending on discount analysis).

<h3>👥 Customer Concentration</h3>

The data shows us that our Top 10 customers vary year-to-year. There is no persistent dominance by the same customers. This may suggests a diversified revenue base rather than heavy concentration risk from top customers. <b>More analysis needed to identify customer spend</b>.

<h3>🏷 Discount Impact</h3>

As to be expected, profit margins decline significantly in relation to our discount tiers. However, these higher discount tiers showed an inverse relation to revenue, this can be due to limited selections. It is worth noting that thour Tier 2 discounts (between 25-50%) showed to have the most negative impact to our profits. Here we see strong sales and negative impact to our profit margin.

<h3>🌍 Regional Performance</h3>

West & East showed to be our strongest regions in sales and profitability. The Central region experienced growth in sales but based on customer spend and profitability this showed to be the region where our customers spent less and we were less profitable. Southern region showed the highest potential for profit growth.

<h3>🔁 Cohort Behavior</h3>

Early cohorts show strong multi-year activity. Retention does not strictly decline year-over-year, but instead we notice a peak drop after the first year and afterwards an increase in strong re-engagament. Revenue from cohorts suggest repeat purchasing behavior.
